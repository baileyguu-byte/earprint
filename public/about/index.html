About Earprint

Conditions, privacy and credits

Earprint is a free, non-commercial prototype for finding EQ settings on the music you actually play. It has no accounts, no ads and no tracking.

Music from Deezer

How song previews can be used

Song search, artist pages, charts and previews come from Deezer’s public API.

  • Previews only. Earprint plays the 30-second clips that Deezer offers to every visitor. For whole songs, open the song on Deezer.
  • For private, non-commercial listening. Under Deezer’s conditions, listening through Earprint is limited to your own private use within your household.
  • No downloads. Previews play inside the page and aren’t saved to your device, and Earprint doesn’t offer them as files.
  • Depends on your country. Deezer decides which songs are available where. Songs it doesn’t offer in your country show as “Not available here”.
  • Independent. Earprint is not affiliated with, sponsored by or endorsed by Deezer. Deezer and the Deezer logo are trademarks of Deezer.

Privacy

What Earprint keeps, and where

  • Nothing about you on a server. Earprint has no accounts and no analytics, and sets no cookies. The only thing it stores on a server is feedback you choose to send.
  • Feedback. Messages sent from the feedback page are kept in Earprint’s own database on Cloudflare with the page you came from and, only if you add them, your contact details and technical details (browser, screen size and Earprint settings, never your age). IP addresses aren’t stored. Only the person who makes Earprint reads them.
  • Saved in your browser only: your settings A and B, your setup (the equaliser layout, your headphone model and, if you enter it, your age), your picked artists, display choices such as the export format, and the measurements of your artists’ songs (ten numbers per song, never any audio). It’s stored with your browser’s local storage, so it stays on this device until you clear it.
  • Talking to Deezer. When you search or play a preview, your browser contacts Deezer directly, so Deezer receives your request and IP address and its privacy policy applies.
  • Your own audio files stay put. Files you open or drop into the lab are decoded inside your browser. They’re never uploaded.
  • Headphone corrections come from this site. Earprint keeps its own copy of the AutoEq results, so searching for your model doesn’t contact AutoEq, GitHub or any measurement site.
  • Hosting. The site is served by Cloudflare, which handles basic connection details such as your IP address to deliver the pages. Fonts are served from this site too, not from a third party.

Credits

What Earprint is built from

  • Export formats. Apple Music, Samsung Galaxy, Spotify, Android, Equalizer APO and Wavelet are named only to show which apps and devices the exports are made for. Their names belong to their owners, and Earprint isn’t connected to any of them.
  • The typical song. Measured by Earprint from 679 previews in November 2026: the 40 highest songs on each of 17 Deezer genre charts, which also serve as the genre comparisons. Only the resulting numbers are kept.
  • Headphone corrections. From AutoEq by Jaakko Pasanen and contributors, used under the MIT licence (licence text): its recommended parametric EQ for each model, as published in July 2025. The measurements behind them are by independent reviewers and labs including oratory1990, crinacle, RTINGS, Innerfidelity, HypetheSonics and Super Review. For other targets and more options, use autoeq.app.
  • Hearing and age. The typical change in hearing with age follows ISO 7029:2017, Acoustics — Statistical distribution of hearing thresholds related to age and gender (Formula 1 and Table 1, the median). The compensation built on it is Earprint’s own.
  • EQ cheat sheet. The instrument ranges follow common mixing practice. For more, read iZotope’s EQ cheat sheet.
  • Fonts. Instrument Sans, JetBrains Mono and Michroma, all under the SIL Open Font License (Instrument Sans, JetBrains Mono, Michroma).

Hearing

Keep the volume sensible

Boosting with an EQ makes it easy to listen louder than you realise, and Earprint isn’t a hearing test. Its age compensation uses the typical hearing for an age, not yours. If you notice changes in your hearing, see an audiologist or your GP.
